Introduction

Much attention has been paid to the rapid economic rise of China—the so-called "China Shock." While global markets and policy experts focus on trade balances and industrial capacity, less attention is often given to the dramatic increase in the standard of living for hundreds of millions of Chinese residents.

The Human Development Index (HDI)

China's rise from a low to high Human Development Index (HDI) in just 20 years is a world first. HDI is a summary measure of average achievement in key dimensions of human development: a long and healthy life, being knowledgeable, and having a decent standard of living.

The index is calculated using three main components: life expectancy at birth, mean and expected years of schooling, and Gross National Income (GNI) per capita.

Foundations: The Mao Years

To understand this growth, we must look at the prerequisite improvements made in earlier decades. During the Mao years (1950–1980), China saw a significant increase in life expectancy, which formed the baseline for later economic growth.

Poverty Reduction (1981–Present)

Since 1981, China has achieved unprecedented success in poverty reduction. This was not simply a passive byproduct of economic growth; specific efforts and policies were implemented to ensure that growth translated into poverty alleviation.

Increasing Educational Attainment

Alongside health and income, education has been a pillar of China's development. Study shows that educational attainment has seen consistent increases across generations.

The Future: Growth and Challenges

What does the future hold? While some imagine a world of "kung-fu robots," real-world projections often focus on the potential slowdown of China's economic growth and the demographic challenges ahead.
